## Session Dashboard

**Optional Cold Open:** [Xavros' History Lesson]()

### One-Sentence Pitch

A respectable school museum field trip becomes a secret race against the clock when the students learn the star exhibit is a monster egg that will hatch before any outside help can arrive.

[[accordion Why the Students Act]]

* **Time pressure:** The Stone starts pulsing at 10:30 p.m. and hatches at midnight. The school is a full day away by normal travel.
* **Teleportation limits:** The school has access to teleportation circles, but the nearest usable circle is not casually available at the museum. Getting a message to the right adult, locating a mage who can cast *teleportation circle*, and authorizing the circle use would take longer than the remaining window.
* **Security risk:** Teleportation circles are powerful but dangerous infrastructure. Museum officials do not publish circle sigils, and Varkenbluff institutions are not going to open emergency teleport access on the word of a disgraced academic.
* **Secrecy:** If the museum leadership learns Dannell has recruited the class, Alda can have them removed, the egg moved, or the story buried. A public alarm could also trap civilians in the building with the Stone.
* **Why no one listens to Dannell:** Casey is brilliant, but over the last few years he has developed a reputation for chasing stranger and less defensible theories. Several recent blunders made colleagues treat him as brilliant-but-unreliable, and his last major dispute with Varkenbluff University burned what credibility he had left. By the time the Murkmire Stone was unearthed and he began researching it independently, most officials were already primed to hear "another Casey Dannell theory" instead of a real warning.
* **Why Dannell chooses them:** Professor David's school is prestigious, and its teachers and students are respected as unusually capable field researchers and problem-solvers. Dannell approaches Miss Elleewick and the students not only because they are present, but because he believes they are more likely than museum officials to take strange evidence seriously and act responsibly under pressure.
* **They are not unsupervised:** Miss Elleewick is present. The adventure can frame this as an ugly but legitimate field judgment: the students are capable, already inside, and the clock makes them the best available option.

[[/accordion]]

[[accordion Run This As]]

1. **Arrival and wonder:** Show the museum as a big cosmopolitan place with dinosaurs, ancient cultures, donors, and school-field-trip energy.
   * Optional lead-in: [Xavros' History Lesson]()
2. **Public introduction:** A docent or tour guide gives the museum-approved story of the Murkmire Stone, and the class gets only a distant, roped-off glimpse.
3. **Quiet warning:** Dr. Dannell pulls Miss Elleewick and the students aside. He has proof, but no institution will listen.
4. **Gala reconnaissance:** The class attends the private preview, meets staff, notices security, and studies the Stone up close.
5. **After-hours heist:** They hide inside or reenter, bypass alarms, evade guards, and swap the Stone with the jade fake.
6. **Countdown complication:** Starting at 10:30, the Stone pulses every 10 minutes and causes weird effects.
7. **Containment handoff:** Get the Stone to Dannell's crystal box. Then decide whether the school takes custody.

[[/accordion]]

## Adventure Flow

1. **Field Trip Arrival**
   * The class arrives after a day's travel to Varkenbluff for a private preview centered on the newly unearthed Murkmire Stone.
   * Let them enjoy a few exhibits before the hook lands.
   * Useful visuals: 
     * [Arrival in Varkenbluff](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/varkenbluff-arrival.png)
     * [Museum Exterior](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/museum-exterior.png)
     * [Class Field Trip](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/class-field-trip.png)
     * [Grand Entrance](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/grand-entrance.png)
   * Music: Start with [Open Road]($load|music|arr/BGM_Field_Safe1.mp3) or [Road Through the Wilds]($load|music|arr/BGM_Field_Gri_02.mp3), then transition to [Museum Crowd]($load|ambience|Restaurant Walla Track-SoundBible.com-479263734.ogg).

2. **Museum Introduction Tour**
   * A young docent gives the public-facing story of the Murkmire Stone while the class gets only a distant look through glass and ropes.
   * This is where the students first hear what the museum wants visitors to believe about the artifact.
   * Useful visual: [Stone Viewed from the Crowd](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/stone-distant-preview.png)

3. **Dannell's Warning**
  * [Dr. Casey Dannell](#dr-casey-dannell) approaches privately, preferably after recognizing Miss Elleewick as a representative of Professor David's prestigious school.
  * He explains that the Murkmire Stone is an egg and will hatch at midnight.
  * He has a crystal box, but it is not ready to travel through the museum; he needs the Stone brought to him.
  * Useful visuals:
    * [Dr. Casey Dannell](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/dr-casey-dannell.png)
    * [Meeting in the Storage Room](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/dannell-storage-room.png)

4. **Gala Reconnaissance**
   * The Gemstone Wing gala runs from 6 p.m. until the museum closes at 8 p.m.
   * The class can study the Stone, meet Alda, notice her clutch, gather gossip, and identify security measures.
   * Key clue: the pedestal will lock the room if the Stone is simply lifted away.
   * Useful visuals: [Gemstone Wing Gala](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/gemstone-wing-gala.png), [Alda Arkin](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/alda-arkin.png), [Maryam Bikram](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/maryam-bikram.png)
   * Music: [Gala Murmur]($load|ambience|crown_murmur_male.mp3)

5. **Security Planning**
   * Strong plans usually involve one or more of these:
     * steal or copy Alda's clutch contents;
     * get spare pass cards from V7;
     * find alarm records in V6;
     * locate the fake jade Stone in V9;
     * use secret passages, attic, basement, or the V12/V13 vent.
   * If the party stalls, have Dannell pass a panicked note: "The egg is warming early. Do not let them lock it away with the guests still nearby."

6. **After-Hours Heist**
   * Once the museum closes, guards patrol, alarms activate, and animated defenses come online.
   * This should feel like stealth, problem-solving, and consequences, not a fair-room combat crawl.
   * Useful visuals: [After-Hours Hallway](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/after-hours-hallway.png), [Prehistoric Predators](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/prehistoric-predators.png), [Curator's Office](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/curators-office.png)
   * Music: [Low Drones]($load|ambience|Industrial_Ambient_Drones_06.mp3)

7. **Countdown and Extraction**
   * At 10:30 p.m., start the Murkmire Stone pulses every 10 minutes.
   * The simplest clean win: swap the Stone with the V9 fake, leave through a planned route, and reach Dannell before midnight.
   * If things go loud, use guard response and the pulse effects to force movement instead of bogging down.
   * Useful visuals: [Stone Pulse](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/stone-pulse.png), [Basement Exit](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/basement-exit.png)
   * Sound: [Heartbeat Loop]($load|effect|heart_beat_loop.ogg)

8. **Containment and Aftermath**
   * Dannell seals the Stone in the crystal box; the pulses stop.
   * Recommended campaign change: Dannell asks Miss Elleewick to take the stone back to the school. "Professor David will know what to do with it."
   * This gives Headmaster David a powerful occult object to "safeguard," fitting the long-term secret arc.

### Scene: Arrival in Varkenbluff

**Purpose:** Establish distance from school, museum spectacle, and why the class is already in the right place.  
**Source Link:** [The Opening Gala](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#kftgv,1,the%20opening%20gala)  
**Visual Aids:**

 * [Arrival in Varkenbluff](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/varkenbluff-arrival.png)
 * [Museum Exterior](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/museum-exterior.png)
 * [Class Field Trip](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/class-field-trip.png)

**Music / Ambience:** Start with [Open Road]($load|music|arr/BGM_Field_Safe1.mp3) or [Road Through the Wilds]($load|music|arr/BGM_Field_Gri_02.mp3), then transition to [Museum Crowd]($load|ambience|Museum Soundscape Rijksmuseum Binaural.mp3)

**Setup:** The class has traveled for the day to Varkenbluff. The field trip is safe on paper: museum exhibits, a chaperoned educational event, and a special preview specifically organized around the newly unearthed Murkmire Stone. The Stone is being promoted as a rare intact Murkmire find: beautiful, mysterious, unlike known local materials, and important enough that scholars still disagree about what it was for.

**Player Goals:**

* Explore the museum.
* Establish what each student is excited or bored by.
* Make Miss Elleewick feel like the responsible adult already present.

[[accordion Other Exhibits to Mention]]

* **The Wilhelmina Hall of Migratory Birds:** Preserved giant marsh birds, elegant wing diagrams, and a stuffed river-heron with an absurdly judgmental face.
* **The William Tallow Cabinet of Curious Shells:** Rows of glossy spiral shells, fossil ammonites, and one hand-sized shell that faintly sings when held to the ear.
* **The Billings Gallery of River Trade:** Old cargo ledgers, model barges, trade-route maps, and a reconstructed dock crane showing how goods move between Varkenbluff and the wetlands.
* **The Willoughby Bones of the Old Marsh:** Partial skeletons from extinct swamp predators, including a long-jawed reptile that looks almost too large to have lived in shallow water.
* **The Sir Wilfred Emberglass Collection of Expedition Relics:** Mud-stained tools, broken camp markers, and fragments of field journals from earlier Murkmire digs.
* **The Saint Willa Shrine Reconstruction:** A careful model of a half-sunken marsh shrine with offerings, lantern niches, and explanatory plaques about local ritual practices.
* **Willosaurus skeleton:** The most intact reconstruction of an ancient marsh predator on the continent.

[[/accordion]]

**Useful Framing Detail:** The trip did not use a teleportation circle. Circle sigils are controlled, expensive, and treated as security-sensitive infrastructure. The school can teleport to trusted circles, but that does not mean every museum, university, or city institution is open to emergency visitors.

**Exit Paths:**

* The class is folded into a small public-facing intro tour about the Murkmire Stone.
* Dr. Dannell intercepts the class immediately afterward.
* Maryam corrals formal guests toward the Gemstone Wing.
* Alda gives a polished curator speech about the Stone.

### Scene: Museum Introduction Tour

**Purpose:** Give the players the official story first, establish the Stone as a distant controlled spectacle, and set up Dannell's contradiction.  
**Source Link:** [The Opening Gala](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#kftgv,1,the%20opening%20gala)  
**Visual Aid:** [Stone Viewed from the Crowd](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/stone-distant-preview.png)  
**Music / Ambience:** [Museum Crowd]($load|ambience|Museum Soundscape (Rijksmuseum) Binaural.mp3)

**Setup:** A young museum docent gathers a small knot of visitors just outside the closed Gemstone Wing. The students get only a distant look from behind ropes and glass, enough to see the Stone's elegant green shape and the attention around it, but not enough to examine it closely. This is the museum's polished version of the story, delivered before any doubt enters the scene.

**Player Goals:**

* Hear the museum-approved narrative.
* Get a first distant look at the Stone.
* Start asking what is known, what is assumed, and what sounds too polished.

[[accordion Tour Guide Introduction to the Murkmire Stone]]

> "If you'll gather just here, you'll be able to see tonight's featured object through the gallery glass."
>
> "The Murkmire Stone was recovered only recently from a carefully documented excavation in the Murkmire wetlands south of Varkenbluff. Even among other significant finds from the region, it is unusually intact, unusually beautiful, and unusually difficult to classify."
>
> "At present, our affiliated experts believe it to be ceremonial in origin, likely tied to a little-understood local funerary or ritual tradition. Its exact purpose remains disputed, which is, of course, part of what makes it such an exciting exhibit."
>
> "The Stone's material has not yet been matched to any common local carving medium, and its surface markings do not neatly align with any single known script or decorative convention. In other words, it is a genuine scholarly puzzle."
> 
> *the crowd starts to murmur in excitement and worry*
>
> "Let me assure you that the Stone has been examined in depth by the top archaeologists here at Varkenbluff, and deemed safe to display to the public, so there's no need to worry."
>
> "This evening's gala guests will be permitted a closer viewing later on, but for now the museum politely asks everyone to appreciate the Stone from behind the ropes and protective glass."

[[/accordion]]

**Exit Paths:**

* Dr. Dannell intercepts the class immediately after the introduction.
* Curious students linger to watch the crowd reaction before moving on.
* A museum staffer ushers guests onward while the Gemstone Wing stays closed until the gala.

### Scene: Dr. Dannell's Warning

**Purpose:** Turn the field trip into an urgent containment problem.  
**Source Link:** [A Cry for Help](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#kftgv,1,a%20cry%20for%20help)  
**Visual Aids:**

 * [Dr. Casey Dannell](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/dr-casey-dannell.png)
 * [Dannell in the Storage Room](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/dannell-storage-room.png)
 * [Murkmire Stone Close-Up](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/murkmire-stone-closeup.png)
 * [Murkmire Stone Close-Up, Group](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/murkmire-stone-closeup-group.png)

**Music / Ambience:** [Creeping Threat]($load|music|arr/BGM_Event_Ascian.mp3)

**Setup:** Dannell is a disgraced anthropologist, exhausted, fraying at the edges, and frighteningly certain he is right. He has already tried university administrators, museum officials, independent research, and a direct theft. Those failures are why he is desperate enough to ask a school group. Ideally he approaches just after the students have heard the museum's official explanation and seen the Stone only from a distance, so his warning lands as a direct contradiction rather than an unrelated interruption.

**How He Makes Contact:** Casey waits until the docent finishes and the crowd starts to drift. He approaches Miss Elleewick first, not the students, and asks for two private minutes in a tone that is controlled but urgent. A good version is: "Miss Elleewick? You're from Professor David's school, yes? I need two minutes, privately. This concerns the Murkmire Stone." If she hesitates, he adds, "I know exactly how this looks. If I'm wrong, you lose two minutes."

**Where the Talk Happens:** He leads them to a cramped museum storage room near the public galleries: shelves, packing straw, spare display stands, draped exhibit shapes, crates, and the smell of dust, canvas, and varnish. It should feel discreet and mildly transgressive, but not like a trap. Casey should never stand between them and the door.

**Why This Group:** Dannell is not asking random children for help. He is asking Miss Elleewick and the students because Professor David's school has a strong reputation for producing unusually capable young adventurers and serious field scholars. In his mind, this is a desperate appeal to the most credible people already in the building, not a reckless first choice.

**Why Officials Dismiss Him:** Casey's warning is not based on a clean test or a mainstream archaeological conclusion. He has been in intermittent correspondence with an old colleague who encouraged him to compare the Stone to obscure fringe material about similar objects. Casey used that line of inquiry to assemble a case from half-forgotten texts, pattern-matching, and signs that could still be argued away by a cautious skeptic. In a better season of his life, colleagues might at least have humored him. Now, after several recent blunders and one last ugly break with the university, most adults hear him as a talented scholar spiraling into another obsessive theory.

**How to Play Dannell Here:** He is deeply uncomfortable asking children for help, even gifted students from a respected school. Play him as ashamed of the ask, trying to appeal first to Miss Elleewick's judgment rather than recruiting the students like heroes. He knows this is a ridiculous and ill-advised request, but his certainty in his own theory is pushing him through the shame. What is going through his head is essentially: "These are the wrong people, and they may also be the only people."

**Conversation Shape:** The scene should move in five short beats: Casey asks Miss Elleewick for privacy; explains why he trusted the school enough to approach; makes the core claim that the Stone is an egg and will hatch tonight; lays out his evidence; then admits, without dodging it, that asking students for help is shameful and absurd, but doing nothing would be worse.

**NPCs Present:**

* [Dr. Casey Dannell](#dr-casey-dannell)
* [Miss Elleewick](/class_1a/class-1a)

**Information and Clues:**

* The Murkmire Stone is an eldritch egg, not a relic.
* It will hatch at midnight.
* It cannot be damaged or destroyed.
* A prepared crystal box can neutralize it, but the box is fragile, still being sealed, and cannot be carried around the museum.
* Publicly raising the alarm risks Alda blocking access, moving the Stone, or trapping civilians inside a cover-up.
* Casey's case began with an exchange of letters from an old colleague who pointed him toward old accounts of similar objects. He followed that lead into obscure material and stitched together a theory that sounds flimsy right up until the Stone starts pulsing.

[[accordion Casey's Initial Explanation and Plea]]

> "Miss Elleewick, I realize how this sounds. I know exactly how this looks. But I am asking you to hear me out before you decide I am mad."
>
> "The object they are calling the Murkmire Stone is not a ceremonial relic. It is not a carved funerary object. It is an egg."
>
> "I believe it will hatch tonight. If it does, it will happen in a crowded museum, after every responsible adult in this building has already convinced themselves there is no danger."
>
> "I have tried the university. I have tried the museum. I have tried every official channel that ought to have listened. They dismissed me, and given my recent history, I cannot even pretend I do not understand why."
>
> "But they are wrong."
>
> "I did not arrive at this because of a single wild guess. I was directed toward a line of comparison by an old colleague, and from there I found older accounts of objects just like this one: misidentified, moved, displayed, and only understood when something living woke inside them."
>
> "I cannot prove it in the neat, respectable way they would require. I can only tell you that too many details are wrong in the same direction, and that if I wait for proof they will accept, it will be too late to matter."
>
> "I have a crystal containment box prepared. If the Stone can be brought to me before midnight, I believe I can neutralize it. If it remains on display, I believe people die."
>
> "I know I should not be asking students for help. I know that. I am asking because you are here, because your school trains people to make field judgments under pressure, and because no one else with both sense and time is available."
>
> "If I am wrong, then I am a disgraced scholar wasting your evening. If I am right, then doing nothing because this request is shameful would be the greater failure."

[[/accordion]]

**Mechanics:**

* Dannell provides a rough museum sketch.
* If asked for proof, he can show recent letters from the old colleague, copied references from obscure comparative sources, matching diagrams, and the first signs that the Stone is warming.
* A DC 13 Arcana, History, Nature, or Religion check confirms his evidence is not solid proof, but it is coherent, serious, and much less wild than museum officials made it sound.
* Fail-forward: even on a failed check, the Stone pulse later proves he was right.

[[accordion If the Students Ask Casey Xavros's Questions]]

**What do you know?**

* The Stone does not fit known regional material culture.
* Its shape, surface furrows, and overall physical character feel wrong for carved ritual stonework.
* The excavation context was suspiciously incomplete; it was found near ritual remains, but not meaningfully integrated with the rest of the site.
* Historical fringe sources describe similar objects that were mistaken for artifacts.
* Those sources repeatedly connect such objects to hatching events, escalating disturbances, and settlements being overwhelmed.
* Crystal containment appears again and again in those accounts as the only reliable neutralization method.

**What are you assuming?**

* That the old colleague put him on the right trail.
* That the obscure sources describe the same class of object.
* That the archaeologists are misreading "dormant" as "inert."
* That the lack of obvious magic does not mean the Stone is harmless.
* That the timeline in the sources is broadly accurate enough to act on now.
* That if he waits for institutionally acceptable proof, it will hatch first.

**How are you so certain it's dangerous?**

* Casey is not certain in the way a university board would demand; he is certain because too many wrong details line up in the same direction.
* He believes the evidence is weak in the academic sense but overwhelming in the practical sense.
* In his mind, the institution has mistaken "nothing has happened yet" for "nothing will happen."
* His strongest argument is risk asymmetry: if he is wrong, they mishandled a stone; if he is right, they put a hatching horror in a public museum.
* Good lines for him:
  * "Because too many things are wrong in the same direction."
  * "It does not behave like a relic. It only resembles one."
  * "You have no proof it is safe. You only have proof that it has not hurt you yet."

[[/accordion]]

**If Things Stall:** Dannell stresses that school adults cannot arrive before midnight. The choice is not "children versus qualified adults"; it is "the people already here versus no one in time."

### Scene: The Gemstone Wing Gala

**Purpose:** Social recon, security clues, and public pressure.  
**Source Link:** [Attending the Gala](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#kftgv,1,attending%20the%20gala)  
**Visual Aid:** [Gemstone Wing Gala](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/gemstone-wing-gala.png), [Murkmire Stone Display](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/murkmire-stone-display.png)  
**Music / Ambience:** [Gala Murmur]($load|ambience|crown_murmur_male.mp3)

**Setup:** Formal dress, donors, gemstone cabinets, a marble pedestal, and the unsettling green Stone at the center. To the public, the Stone is a prestige artifact: a newly excavated puzzle from the Murkmire that may rewrite part of local history, or so the museum hopes. This is the students' first close and socially legitimate access to the Stone and the affiliated archaeologists after the museum's earlier distant preview.

**NPCs Present:**

* [Maryam Bikram](#maryam-bikram)
* [Alda Arkin](#alda-arkin)
* Donors and gala guests

**Player Goals:**

* Study the Stone and pedestal.
* Get gossip.
* Notice Alda's clutch.

[[accordion If the Students Ask the Museum / Archaeologists Xavros's Questions]]

Use these answers from Alda, museum staff, or affiliated archaeologists. They should sound professional, reasonable, and incomplete, not foolish.

**What do you know?**

* The Murkmire Stone was recovered from a Murkmire excavation layer associated with ritual or funerary remains.
* It is unusually dense, nonmagical by standard testing, and resistant to damage.
* Its material does not match common local stone, ceramic, bone, or metal.
* Its furrows appear deliberate, but do not clearly match any known script or iconographic tradition.
* The Stone has been kept in isolation for the last week under controlled observation.
* During that week, tests found no curses, no unstable magic, no detectable life signs, and no other recognized danger indicators.
* It remained inert during excavation, transport, quarantine observation, and routine handling.
* Multiple recognized experts have reviewed the findings and deemed it safe to display.

**What are they assuming?**

* The Stone is a ceremonial, symbolic, or funerary object rather than a living or active one.
* Its strangeness reflects a lost local culture, foreign trade contact, or an unknown ritual tradition.
* If it were truly dangerous, standard magical screening or controlled observation would already have shown some warning sign.
* Dr. Dannell's interpretation is another speculative leap beyond the available evidence.

**Why did they conclude it was safe to move and display?**

* It behaved like an inert artifact under every standard test they trust.
* It showed no aura that suggested an active magical threat.
* The museum followed its own procedures for unusual finds, including isolation and expert review.
* The staff believe the real risks are theft, mishandling, and public embarrassment, not imminent supernatural danger.
* By the time Casey raised stronger objections, the institution already had professional consensus and exhibition momentum behind the display.

[[/accordion]]

[[accordion If the Students Ask What the Dig Site Was Like]]

Use this as a concise museum-side answer. It should make the site feel real without implying that the players need to solve a second mystery there tonight.

> "A low ruin mound in the Murkmire, partly swallowed by reeds and blackwater. Mostly collapsed stonework, weathered funerary fragments, and the sort of ritual debris we see in a dozen minor sites every decade."
>
> "We believed it to be a small ceremonial or burial site, not a major settlement. The Stone was recovered from a sealed lower layer beneath the more ordinary remains."
>
> "In truth, the site itself was unremarkable. The object was what made it important."

Useful paraphrase if the students press for specifics:

* damp excavation trenches
* broken pottery and grave goods
* some carved stones and low walls
* no dramatic curse site, no great temple, no obvious monster lair
* the Stone was the anomaly, not the ruin around it

### Dr. Casey Dannell

**Role:** Discredited anthropologist, quest giver, containment expert  
**Appears In:** Dannell's warning, containment aftermath  
**Wants:** The Stone safely sealed before midnight and his warnings taken seriously.  
**Knows:** Murkmire Stone is an egg, hatches at midnight, cannot be destroyed, crystal containment works, and an old colleague's correspondence is what first pointed him toward the right fringe sources.  
**Personality:** Brilliant, anxious, proud, increasingly unreliable in presentation, humiliated by institutional dismissal, and starting to look a little unhinged even when he is right.  
**Why He Was Dismissed:** Casey has spent the last few years drifting further into speculative theories and recently made several public blunders. His final break with Varkenbluff University was bad enough that when the Murkmire Stone appeared, administrators were already predisposed to hear him as a disgraced crank instead of a serious warning voice.  
**How They Treat Students:** With more respect than is probably comfortable; he knows asking children is unreasonable, but time has made every better option fail.  
**Useful Table Detail:** His hands shake when he opens his notes, and he smells faintly of drink, stress, and sleeplessness, but his voice steadies when he explains the evidence. He is visibly relieved to see representatives of Professor David's school, then immediately guilty that relief is attached to asking children for help.  
**Secret Campaign Detail:** The "old colleague" in Casey's letters is Professor David, though the exact shape of that relationship can stay flexible. David's goal in this adventure is for the students to return the Murkmire Stone to the school without revealing his own hand too early.  
**Visual / Stat Block:** [Portrait](^class_1a/murkmire_malevolence/dr-casey-dannell.png), [5e.tools stat block](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#dr.%20cassee%20dannell_kftgv)

[[accordion Casey's Full Reasoning If Pressed]]

**What Casey admits he actually knows:**

* The Stone does not fit known regional material culture.
* The object's physical details are wrong for ordinary carved ritual stonework.
* Comparable fringe accounts describe similar objects later recognized as dormant eldritch eggs.
* Crystal containment is the one repeated neutralization method in those sources.

**What Casey is assuming:**

* That the old colleague put him on the right trail.
* That the obscure sources describe the same kind of object.
* That the archaeologists are overvaluing the absence of obvious danger signs.
* That the timeline in the old accounts is close enough to act on now.

**Why he still pushes so hard:**

* He thinks the institution has mistaken "nothing has happened yet" for "nothing will happen."
* He believes the evidence is weak in the academic sense but overwhelming in the practical sense.
* He sees this as a field judgment under uncertainty, not a thesis defense.
* In his mind, the cost of being wrong is tiny compared with the cost of doing nothing if he is right.

## Campaign Adaptation Notes

**School Framing:** This is a planned museum field trip that overlaps with the special gala preview for the Murkmire Stone. The class is not sent to steal anything; the dangerous part is accidental.

**Why the Students Are Involved:** They are physically present, gifted, supervised by Miss Elleewick, able to act before midnight, and specifically trusted because they come from Professor David's respected school.

**Why Adult NPCs Do Not Solve It:** The school is a day's travel away, teleportation circle use is rare and controlled, the museum is not going to open a security-sensitive route on short notice, and official local adults already dismissed Dannell as a disgraced scholar pushing another fringe theory.

**Tone Adjustments:** Keep the opening bright and school-trip-like. Let the horror emerge slowly through warmth, pulsing light, intrusive whispers, and institutional denial.

**Level / Balance Adjustments:** Emphasize stealth, alarms, procedures, and time. Do not upscale every guard into a lethal combatant. If combat becomes necessary, use max HP for defensive constructs, add guard waves, or use the environment instead of inflating all stat blocks.

**Continuity Links:** The Stone is an ideal object for the headmaster to take into school custody. In the moment that looks responsible; later it can read as part of his pattern of collecting powerful objects through the students.
