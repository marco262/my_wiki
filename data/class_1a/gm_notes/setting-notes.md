# Setting Notes

Use this page for general setting ideas, worldbuilding assumptions, and continuity notes that are likely to matter across multiple adventures.

## Magic Infrastructure

### Teleportation Circles

The world is high-magic and in the early phases of globalization. Permanent teleportation circles exist across the world and can transport people or goods instantly, assuming a sufficiently powerful mage is available and knows the destination circle's sigil sequence.

Teleportation circles are rare but regularly used by large cities, wealthy institutions, and powerful organizations, including Professor David's School for Precocious Heroes.

They remain sparse for several reasons:

* **Cost:** Permanent circles require significant time, materials, skilled labor, and magical expertise. 
  * Specifically, 365 straight days of a high-level arcane spellcaster casting a 5th-level spell, spending 50+ gp every day, for a total cost of 18,250+ gp per Teleportation Circle erected, plus the cost of construction, high-skill labor, and ongoing maintenance and security.
  * 1 gp ~= $100, so the spellcasting component cost of the circle alone is over $1.8M, with the total easily exceeding $10M.
* **Availability:** A working circle is only useful when someone can cast _[Teleportation Circle](/dnd/spell/Teleportation Circle)_ or operate comparable institutional magic.
* **Security:** A circle is an access point. Any mage who can cast the spell and knows a circle's sigils can open a portal to that location.
* **Political control:** Cities and institutions guard circle sigils carefully. Sharing them is a matter of trust, treaty, emergency authority, or espionage.

For adventure framing, this means teleportation can be common enough to shape trade and politics and provide easy access to distant locations when needed for the story, but restricted enough that "call an adult from school" is not automatically practical. A location can be only a day's travel away and still unreachable by school authorities in the time needed to solve an urgent local problem.

## Campaign Premise

The Class 1A students are at Professor David's school because each has unusual gifts that make them especially suited to heroing. The school trains them to become great adventurers, but the students are still pre-teens and teens, so responsible adults generally avoid deliberately sending them into serious danger.

Most adventures should therefore begin as one of two patterns:

* **Low-risk assignment:** A field trip, supervised errand, test, or training exercise that should be safe on paper.
* **Accidental escalation:** The students are present when a situation becomes urgent, dangerous, or too time-sensitive for other help to arrive.

Secret long-term truth: Headmaster David is an Archfey grooming powerful children to become his personal army and to collect powerful objects, monsters, favors, and alliances. This should remain subtext until much later.
