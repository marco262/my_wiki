# Adventure Note Template

Use this structure for GM-facing adventure notes. The goal is to make the page useful at the table without rewriting the full adventure. Link to the source adventure for complete room text, boxed text, and full stat blocks when possible.

Do not add `toc: false` to detailed GM adventure notes by default. A table of contents is usually useful for jumping between scenes, NPCs, monsters, locations, and prep sections.

## Design Goals

- Put the most commonly needed information near the top.
- Keep the expected plot path in one clear outline.
- Keep reusable details, like NPCs, monsters, and locations, in dedicated sections.
- Put visual aid and sound links where they are most useful during play, usually in the relevant scene, NPC, monster, or location entry.
- Use a top-level asset section only for global/reusable assets or source links that are useful across multiple scenes.
- In scene notes, link to reusable sections instead of repeating the same information.
- Treat mechanics notes as table prompts: what to roll, what happens on success, what happens on failure, and how the story keeps moving.
- Keep source links close to the scenes or locations where they are needed.

# Adventure Flow

Use this as the expected plot outline. Keep it short enough to scan while running.

1. **Opening Hook**
   - What gets the party involved.
   - Which NPCs or locations matter.
   - What the players should understand by the end of the beat.

2. **Inciting Problem**
   - What goes wrong or becomes urgent.
   - What choice or objective is placed in front of the party.

3. **Investigation / Approach**
   - Main leads.
   - Optional leads.
   - What happens if the party stalls or misses a clue.

4. **Complication**
   - The pressure that changes the situation.
   - New information, danger, countdown, rival action, or moral choice.

5. **Main Challenge / Dungeon / Heist**
   - Primary objective.
   - Obstacles.
   - Fail-forward options.

6. **Finale**
   - Trigger.
   - Main encounter or decision.
   - Stakes at the table.

7. **Aftermath**
   - Outcomes.
   - Rewards.
   - Consequences.
   - Hooks into later adventures.
